# VSS Beam Sweep — Whisper vs Moonshine vs Zipformer (Vietnamese)

**Date:** 2026-07-30  
**Dataset:** VietSuperSpeech (VSS) — 100 clean Vietnamese utterances per split, 10–15 s, conversational podcast speech (Vietcetera/HaveASip channels)  
**Manifests:** `eval_data/vss_vi_test_manifest.json` (test), `eval_data/vss_vi_validation_manifest.json` (validation)  
**Candidates:** `whisper-small-multilang-ct2-cpu`, `moonshine-tiny-vi-hf-cpu`, `zipformer-vi-30m-sherpa-onnx-cpu`  
**Metric:** WER (case-normalized), RTF, latency, peak RAM  
**Prior benchmark for comparison:** [FLEURS vi sweep](beam-sweep-asr-whisper.md) / [beam-sweep-asr-zipformer.md](beam-sweep-asr-zipformer.md)

---

## ⚠️ Headline Finding: VSS is NOT a valid benchmark for Zipformer-30M-VI

**Both VSS test AND validation splits are contaminated.** Zipformer-30M-VI produces byte-for-byte identical transcripts for 64–65% of utterances on both splits:

| Split | Zipformer norm-exact | Whisper norm-exact | Moonshine norm-exact |
|-------|---------------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Test | **64%** | 0% | 1% |
| Validation | **65%** | — | — |

(Case-insensitive + punctuation-normalized comparison.)

The validation split was expected to be clean held-out data (0 file-path overlap with test), but the exact-match rate is identical to the test split. Two possible explanations:

1. **Reference generation bias** — VSS references are ALL-CAPS with no punctuation, which matches Zipformer's output style. The community-built transcripts were likely machine-generated by a Zipformer-family ASR. Zipformer "recognizes" them exactly because it agrees with its own transcription style, while Whisper/Moonshine transcribe in their own style (mixed case, punctuation, different tokenization).
2. **Training data overlap at source level** — Zipformer-30M-VI was trained on 6,000 h of conversational Vietnamese including the Vietcetera/HaveASip channels; validation segments from those videos may still appear in training.

Either way, **VSS cannot be used to compare Zipformer against other models.** Its VSS WER numbers are inflated artifacts. FLEURS (no source overlap, standardized test set) remains the authoritative cross-model benchmark.

| 2 | 0.139 | 0.133 | 0.48 | 0.036 | 527 | 41% |
| 4 | 0.152 | 0.159 | 0.63 | 0.047 | 526 | 39% |
| 5 | 0.161 | 0.159 | 0.62 | 0.047 | 527 | 38% |
| 8 | 0.158 | 0.153 | 0.74 | 0.055 | 526 | 38% |

**Do not use these WER numbers for Zipformer quality assessment.** The greedy (beam=1) "best" result is an artifact: greedy reproduces memorized/reference-style transcripts verbatim (65% exact), while beam search drifts away from them (38–41% exact).

**Trustworthy Zipformer numbers (FLEURS, no overlap):** WER 0.137–0.146 (vi), beam search gains ≤0.01 → **beam=1 greedy is the production choice.**

**Contrast with FLEURS:** Whisper WER was flat (~0.45) across beams on FLEURS read speech, but improves monotonically on VSS conversational/code-switched speech (1.13 → 0.75 at beam=8, −33%). If production input is conversational with code-switching, Whisper needs beam ≥ 4–8; if read speech, beam=1 suffices.

**Caveat:** Whisper's high absolute WER on VSS (0.75–1.13) is partly reference-style mismatch — VSS references are machine-generated uppercase text, which penalizes Whisper's mixed-case punctuated output beyond genuine errors. The direction (beam helps) is reliable; the magnitude is not.

## Conclusions

1. **VSS is unusable for Zipformer benchmarking.** Both test and validation splits show 64–65% byte-exact agreement — reference-generation bias or training-source overlap. Any Zipformer vs Whisper/Moonshine comparison on VSS is invalid.

2. **FLEURS remains the only trustworthy cross-model benchmark:**

| Model | Vi WER (FLEURS) | Beam decision |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Zipformer 30M** | **0.137** | beam=1 (greedy) |
| Whisper Small | 0.452 | beam=1 (flat) |
| Moonshine Tiny | 0.524 | beam=1 (flat) |

3. **Beam-search direction findings on VSS are usable for Whisper** (0% exact = no reference bias for it): conversational code-switched speech benefits from beam ≥ 4–8, unlike read speech where beam=1 suffices.

4. **Moonshine remains not viable** — worst WER, RAM grows with beam, no CT2 path, latency explodes at beam=4+.

5. **For ADR-008:** Zipformer's production WER claims must cite FLEURS (or a future truly held-out conversational set), never VSS. A genuinely clean conversational benchmark would require audio the model has never seen (e.g., Kavi's own recorded factory/logistics speech).

---

## Caveats & Limitations

- **Zipformer contamination applies to both splits** — switching to the VSS validation set did NOT resolve the leakage (65% exact vs 64% on test). Both were expected to be independent; neither is clean for this model.
- **Whisper/Moonshine VSS numbers also carry reference-style mismatch** — machine-generated ALL-CAPS references inflate their WER. Their relative ordering (Whisper < Moonshine) is reliable; absolute values are not.
- **VSS has no noise conditions** — clean audio only; FLEURS mixed-noise sweeps remain the source for SNR behavior.
- **Moonshine beam=4 latency anomaly** — single-point spike, exclude from latency analysis.
- **Whisper outliers** — items with WER > 5 (hallucination/repetition loops) skew the mean.
